About the Project

Preventing amputations through evidence-based, cost-effective foot care

Diabetic foot complications, including ulcers, infections and amputations, represent one of the most devastating and costly consequences of diabetes. In Belgium, thousands of patients face lower limb amputations each year, many of which are preventable with the right interventions.

The PARADISE project (Towards Pressure-Adherence & Education Driven Service in Diabetic Foot Disease ) takes an interdisciplinary approach. By combining biomechanical analysis with health economic evaluation, we identify the most effective and cost-efficient strategies for diabetic foot care in Belgian clinical practice.

About PARADISE

What does PARADISE stand for?

Pressure-Adherence & Education Research And Diabetic foot disease → Integrated Service & Education

This PhD project is funded and hosted at KU Leuven Campus Bruges. It combines quantitative biomechanical assessment with health economic modelling to identify the most effective and cost-efficient care pathways for patients at risk of diabetic foot complications.

The project runs in close collaboration with Belgian diabetic foot clinics and builds directly on existing Belgian consensus guidelines developed in partnership with the BDFPG.

Instrumentation

Data Collection Instruments

The PARADISE project relies on four validated measurement systems to objectively assess physical activity, adherence and plantar pressure in patients with diabetic foot pathology.

Co-primary endpoints
  1. Foot-ulcer recurrence over 18 months
  2. Footwear adherence ≥80% of average activity profile, 7 days/week; primary analysis at 12 months
Offloading target

Peak pressure < 200 kPa or ≥ 25% reduction vs. baseline

Plantaire drukanalyse · Studieteam

Tekscan F-Scan Go

The Tekscan F-Scan Go is a portable, in-shoe plantar pressure measurement system that uses thin, flexible sensor insoles to capture dynamic pressure distribution across the entire plantar surface during walking. It provides spatial pressure maps, peak pressure values, pressure-time integrals and force data in real time.

In the PARADISE project, the F-Scan Go is central to our biomechanical assessment. It enables identification of high-risk pressure regions, evaluation of offloading footwear effectiveness and longitudinal tracking of pressure changes over the course of treatment. Measurements are performed by the Study team; Usual Care results remain blinded.

Novel pedar

The Novel pedar is a wireless in-shoe pressure measurement system: thin sensor insoles worn inside the participant’s own footwear, linked to the novel database software. Within the PARADISE project the pedar provides an independent in-shoe reference alongside the F-Scan Go. It will replace the F-Scan Go over time and currently runs in parallel for cross-validation. Insole calibration is checked with the trublu calibration device at least every 3 months.

Measurements are performed by the Care team (DFC). The pedar was added to the protocol after the initial design phase and is currently running in parallel with the F-Scan Go for comparative purposes.

Activiteitsprofiel · Zorgteam (DFC)

McRoberts DynaPort

The McRoberts DynaPort (MoveMonitor) is a validated wearable accelerometer worn at the lower back that provides detailed classification of postures and transitions, step counts and walking-bout characteristics during free-living conditions. Worn for a full week at baseline and at 6 months; not waterproof.

In the PARADISE project, the DynaPort delivers the objective activity profile used to define the 80%-adherence benchmark for the co-primary endpoint. Measurements are performed by the Care team (DFC).

Orthotimer

The Orthotimer is a small temperature sensor embedded in the custom made foot orthosis (CMFO). It logs temperature at 15-minute intervals with a battery life of approximately 100 days, and is read out and replaced every 3 months by the Care team (DFC).

Within the PARADISE project, the Orthotimer objectively quantifies how long patients actually wear their prescribed orthosis — a key determinant of healing outcomes. Patients are informed that it is “their sensor” and that it records only temperature (no GPS, audio, or camera).
